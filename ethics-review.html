Administrative Screening

The Secretariat's first pass — a completeness check, not an ethics decision.

Completeness

Every required field, form and attachment is present before a reviewer ever sees the file.

Applicant Identity

Principal investigator and research-team identity and institutional affiliation are verified.

Institutional Information

Affiliation, supervisor endorsement and site permissions are checked where applicable.

Required Attachments

Protocol, consent materials, instruments and technical annexes match what the study needs.

Payment Status

Fee status is confirmed where a review fee applies — it never substitutes for ethical review.

Scope Check

Confirms the submission genuinely falls within MSREC's remit before it proceeds further.

Not a Decision

Screening never makes a substantive ethics judgement — that authority belongs to reviewers and the Committee alone.

"Incomplete" State

Missing information returns the application to the applicant with a structured checklist until corrected.

Reviewer Assignment

Matched by expertise, cleared for independence, before a single protocol page is opened.

Reviewer assignment is based on subject expertise, current workload and independence from the applicant. Before a reviewer can open the full protocol, they must first confirm whether an actual, potential or perceived conflict of interest exists.

Where MSREC policy adopts a blinded or non-disclosure model, reviewer identity may remain confidential to the applicant throughout the process.

Assessment Domains

Every protocol is measured against the same eight domains, whatever the discipline.

Scientific & Social Value

Is the research question meaningful, and is the design capable of producing useful knowledge?

Participant Selection

Are inclusion, exclusion and recruitment fair and appropriately justified?

Risk & Benefit

Are foreseeable harms minimized and proportionate to anticipated benefits or knowledge value?

Consent

Is information understandable, voluntary and appropriate to the participant population?

Privacy & Confidentiality

Are data collection, access, sharing, retention and disclosure risks adequately controlled?

Vulnerability

Are additional safeguards appropriate for children, dependent relationships or other vulnerable groups?

AI & Digital Systems

Are data provenance, bias, safety, cybersecurity, explainability and human oversight addressed?

Conflicts & Funding

Could financial, institutional or personal interests distort participant protection or reporting?

Decision Categories

Five possible outcomes, each with a defined meaning and a defined system action.

Approved

Protocol satisfies MSREC requirements, subject to any stated conditions of approval.

Approval letter generated; post-approval obligations activate

Approved with Conditions

Approval is granted, but specific conditions must be met or observed.

Conditions recorded; activation restricted until pre-implementation items are met

Modifications Required

Changes must be made before the protocol can receive final approval.

Structured comments returned; revision workflow opens

Deferred

Substantial information or further Committee deliberation is required first.

Decision held pending specified information or additional review

Not Approved

The protocol is not ethically acceptable in its current form.

Reasoned decision issued, with appeal information where permitted

Appeals & Researcher Responses

Two different mechanisms, kept deliberately distinct.

Responding to Modifications

  • Answer each reviewer or Committee comment individually.
  • Describe the specific change made in response.
  • Upload a tracked or clean revised document, as MSREC policy requires.
  • Routine, and available on any protocol returned for changes.

Formal Appeals

  • Permitted only under MSREC's Appeals Policy.
  • Distinguished from a routine response to modifications.
  • Cannot be used to bypass a substantive, unresolved ethical concern.
  • Reserved for genuine procedural or decision-level disputes.

Committee Meetings

How a review actually reaches a Committee decision.

Full Committee review happens only when quorum is met. Members with a declared conflict cannot contribute to that decision and, where the conflict policy requires it, are not counted toward quorum at all. Meeting records capture attendance, recusals, the protocol identifier, the key ethical issues and the final decision — without reproducing confidential participant information.

Quorum is calculated after recusals — a decision cannot proceed without it
Members are prompted to declare conflicts on every agenda item before deliberation
The public calendar never discloses confidential protocol titles or applicant details
Approved minutes are stored securely with full version history retained
See the full meeting schedule, quorum rules and format on the Board & Committee page

Post-Approval Management

An approved study is an active record with obligations — not a completed transaction.

Amendments

Review before implementing a material change to an approved protocol, outside of immediate safety actions.

Continuing Review

Confirms a study's ongoing ethical acceptability and whether its approval should continue.

Progress Reports

A short annual report — mostly pre-populated — covering status, recruitment and any changes.

Deviations

Documents any departure from the approved protocol, its cause and the corrective action taken.

Adverse Events

Escalates participant safety issues or serious unexpected problems as soon as they arise.

Suspension

Controls a study that should not continue under its existing approval, with participant protections.

Study Closure

Formally closes an approved record once a study's active obligations have been met.

Final Reports

Captures completion date, final sample, outstanding events, data status and study outputs.
